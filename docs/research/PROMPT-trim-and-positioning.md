# Research brief: trim size and positioning for a three-volume Panchadasi set

You have web access. I do not. I need cursory but concrete market research
on Amazon (and anywhere else useful) to settle two decisions. Work from what
is actually listed and selling, not from general publishing advice.

## What we are making

Three public-domain occult titles by **Swami Panchadasi** (a pen name of
William Walker Atkinson), to be sold as a matched three-volume set as well as
individually:

1. **The Human Aura: Astral Colors and Thought Forms** — Advanced Thought
   Publishing Co., Chicago, 1916. Its title page calls it "Volume I of the
   Occult Manuals", so a series identity already exists in the source.
2. **Clairvoyance and Occult Powers** — same author, 1916.
3. A third Panchadasi title, not yet fixed. Candidates are *The Astral
   World* and *Genuine Mediumship, or The Invisible Powers*. **Part of your
   job is to tell me which third title makes the strongest commercial set** —
   which is most searched for, least well served by existing editions, and
   most coherent with the other two.

These are careful editions, not scan-dumps: the text is transcribed from the
original printing, checked against independent readings, compositor's errors
raised as editorial queries and ruled on individually, and each volume gets
an editor's introduction, a note on the text, and (for at least one) a
glossary. That is the thing to differentiate on, and the positioning question
below is largely "how do you make that legible on a product page".

## Hard constraints, already measured

We lay the books out ourselves, so page count is a lever we control. Measured
page counts from our own engine, body plus front matter, **before** the
editor's introduction and glossary are added (those will add roughly 10–20
pages to each):

| Trim | Body size | The Human Aura | Clairvoyance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 × 9 | 11 pt | 50 | 244 |
| 6 × 9 | 12 pt | 65 | 285 |
| 5.5 × 8.5 | 11 pt | 61 | 286 |
| 5.5 × 8.5 | 12 pt | 70 | 332 |
| 5.25 × 8 | 12 pt | 75 | 380 |
| 5 × 8 | 11 pt | 72 | 337 |
| 5 × 8 | 12 pt | **81** | 388 |

**The binding constraint is the spine.** KDP will not print text on a spine
below 79 pages. *The Human Aura* is a short book, and at 6 × 9 it cannot have
a printed spine at all. For a set meant to sit together on a shelf and be
recognised as a set, that matters more than almost anything else — so please
confirm the current KDP spine-text minimum and tell me if I have it wrong.

## What I need you to find out

### 1. Trim size

- What trim sizes do the **existing** editions of these three titles actually
  use? List the ones you can verify, with page counts and prices.
- What trim do the *better-regarded* public-domain occult reprints use —
  the ones with real reviews and complaints about quality absent? Look at
  publishers who do this well and name them.
- Is there a trim that reads as "serious edition" rather than "print-on-demand
  filler" to this audience? Say what the visual cue actually is: page size,
  paper colour, thickness, spine, typography.
- Given the table above, **recommend one trim for all three volumes** and say
  what it costs us. If the recommendation forces a body size or added matter
  to clear the spine threshold, say so explicitly.

### 2. Differentiation

The market for these titles is crowded with cheap scan-reprints. I want to
know precisely what the bad ones do badly, from the evidence:

- Read the **1- and 2-star reviews** of existing editions of all three titles.
  What do buyers actually complain about? Quote them. This is the single most
  useful thing you can bring back.
- What do the few good editions charge, and what do they visibly do to earn it?
- How are the good ones described — subtitle conventions, series naming, what
  goes in the A+ content and the description's first two lines?
- Is there an existing matched *set* of Panchadasi or Atkinson titles on the
  market? If so, what does it look like and what does it charge? If not, say
  so — an unserved set is the opening.

### 3. Cover art

- What do the covers of the editions that sell actually look like? Describe
  the pattern rather than one example.
- What would make a three-volume set read as a set at thumbnail size, which
  is where the buying decision happens?
- Rough cost and sourcing for commissioned cover art at this level — a
  designer, a stock-illustration route, and a public-domain-artwork route,
  with what each realistically costs for three matched covers.

### 4. Price

- What do comparable editions charge, by page count and trim?
- What does KDP's printing cost come to at the recommended trim for a 90-page
  and a 390-page book, and what does that leave at plausible list prices?
- Is a boxed or bundled set worth doing on KDP, or is a "series" listing the
  practical route?

## How to answer

- **Evidence first.** For every claim, give the listing, the price, the page
  count, the trim, and the review quote you are drawing on. I would rather
  have six verified listings than twenty asserted ones.
- **Say what you could not verify.** Amazon hides trim size on some listings;
  where you are inferring, say you are inferring.
- **End with a single recommendation** — one trim, one body size, one
  positioning line, one price point per volume — and the two strongest
  arguments against it.
- Do not reproduce or closely imitate any existing cover; I want to know what
  the field looks like so as to sit apart from it, not inside it.

## Also worth knowing, if it is cheap to find out

- Whether "Swami Panchadasi" or "William Walker Atkinson" is the better name
  to put on the cover, by search volume and by what buyers of these titles
  seem to look for.
- Whether these titles have any trademark or estate complications. They should
  all be public domain in the US (published 1915–1919), but confirm.
